# EWMA VaR Evaluation — 12/08/2026

## 1. Objective

This evaluation compares the canonical EWMA VaR baseline with the established Historical Simulation baseline on exactly the same one-day-ahead target dates. The analysis uses the equal-weight HPG/FPT/MWG portfolio and `portfolio_simple_return`.

The purpose is to measure out-of-sample VaR behavior under a common evaluation contract, not to tune EWMA parameters or establish formal statistical superiority.

## 2. Evaluation contract

The comparison uses the following fixed settings:

- VaR horizon: one trading day.
- Confidence level: 95%.
- Alpha: 0.05.
- VaR convention: `VaR = max(0, -q_0.05)`.
- Violation rule: `target_return < quantile_return`.
- Historical Simulation baseline window: 250 observations.
- EWMA decay: 0.94.
- EWMA conditional mean assumption: zero.
- EWMA innovation distribution: standard Normal.
- Common evaluation sample: 1,387 target dates from 2020-12-31 through 2026-07-28.

Historical forecasts are read from the existing canonical Historical backtest. EWMA forecasts are generated by the production `ewma_var_forecast` implementation.

## 3. No-look-ahead validation

For every EWMA forecast, the return history ends at `forecast_date`, and every observation supplied to the EWMA recursion is strictly earlier than `target_date`. The realized target return is therefore excluded from the forecasting history.

The contract was first checked independently at the first, middle and final forecast origins. The complete evaluation notebook then applies the same assertions to all 1,387 forecast rows.

The realized `portfolio_simple_return` at each target date was also compared with the canonical Historical `target_return`. The maximum observed alignment difference was zero within the numerical checks used for this analysis.

These dates are reported only as empirical exception observations. No market-event cause is assigned without a separate evidence source.

## 6. Interpretation

EWMA's violation rate of 5.263157895% is descriptively closer to the nominal 5% level than the Historical Simulation rate of 5.407354001%. EWMA also has the lower Pinball Loss on the common evaluation sample and a slightly lower Average VaR.

The observed EWMA VaR range is wider. Its minimum VaR is lower than the Historical minimum, while its maximum VaR reaches 5.666074912%, compared with 4.378556664% for Historical Simulation. This indicates that the two methods can generate materially different time-varying risk thresholds even though most exception dates overlap.

These findings are descriptive. They do not establish that EWMA is statistically or universally superior to Historical Simulation. No formal coverage hypothesis test is used in this comparison, so no statistical rejection, significance or formal model-failure conclusion is made.

The canonical EWMA decay remains 0.94. The test sample is not used to tune lambda.
